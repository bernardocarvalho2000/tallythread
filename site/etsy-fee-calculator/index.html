How Etsy fees work

Etsy doesn't take one fee per sale. It takes several small ones, each calculated on a slightly different amount. Here is what each line in the calculator means.

Listing fee

Every listing costs $0.20 to publish and stays live for four months. When an item sells, Etsy automatically renews the listing and charges another $0.20 — so in practice you pay $0.20 for each item sold. If a buyer orders three of the same item, that's three renewals, or $0.60. Sellers outside the US are billed the equivalent in their own currency.

Transaction fee

Etsy charges 6.5% of the total you charge the buyer: the item price plus shipping and gift wrapping, after any discount. Many sellers forget that shipping is included. Charging $5 for shipping costs you about $0.33 in transaction fees, even if the label costs you the full $5.

Payment processing fee

If you use Etsy Payments, there's a processing fee on the full order total. It depends on where your bank account is: in the US it's 3% plus $0.25 per order; in the UK it's 4% plus £0.20; in most of the EU, 4% plus €0.30; in Canada, 3% plus C$0.25; in Australia, 3% plus A$0.25. The fixed part is charged once per order, not per item, which is why small orders lose a bigger share to fees.

Regulatory operating fee

Sellers based in the UK, France, Italy, Spain, Canada, Türkiye, India, Vietnam and Hungary pay an extra percentage on each order to cover local regulation costs. The rate differs by country and has changed over time, so the calculator leaves it at 0% until you enter your current rate from Etsy's fee page.

Offsite Ads fee

Etsy advertises listings on Google, social media and other sites. If a buyer clicks one of those ads and buys from your shop within 30 days, you pay an Offsite Ads fee on the order total: 15% for most shops, or a reduced 12% for shops that made more than $10,000 in the past 12 months (those shops can't opt out). The fee is capped at $100 per order. It's the one that can quietly erase the profit on a sale, so it's worth checking how your prices hold up with it switched on.

A worked example

A US seller sells one item for $25 and charges $5 shipping. The item costs $6 to make and the shipping label costs $4.50. The order total is $30.

Listing fee (1 item × $0.20)$0.20
Transaction fee (6.5% × $30)$1.95
Processing fee (3% × $30 + $0.25)$1.15
Total Etsy fees$3.30
Item cost + shipping label$10.50
Net profit ($30 − $3.30 − $10.50)$16.20
Margin ($16.20 ÷ $30)54%

Now say the same sale came through Offsite Ads at 15%. That adds 15% × $30 = $4.50 in fees. Total fees rise to $7.80 and net profit drops to $11.70, a 39% margin. One setting, and nearly a third of the profit is gone.

That's why the calculator also gives you a price for a target margin. Enter the margin you want and it works backwards through every percentage fee to find the item price that gets you there.
